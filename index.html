How it works

  1. Pick a place: click anywhere on the map, or try a case below it.
  2. We grab 40+ years of NASA climate records for that spot.
  3. We test 10 climate variables (like nighttime temperature, rainfall, and wind) for a statistically clear trend, not just an unusual year.
  4. Select any result for a plain-language summary, a chart, and how confident we are.

How we decide if something is "really changing": Weather bounces around every year, so one hot summer doesn't prove anything. We look at 40+ years at once and use two standard science tools: the Mann-Kendall test (does it keep going the same direction, more than random luck would?) and Sen's slope (how fast?). We use yearly averages so normal summer/winter swings don't fool us.

Honest note: We check 10 things at once, so a "Possible trend" result could occasionally be a false alarm. And these results show what changed, not why.
